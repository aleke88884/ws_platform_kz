## Questions and answers
The same five questions were asked in every interview ([Interview questions](interview-questions.md)).

**Q1. Could you tell us how you learn C# or prepare for WorldSkills (or how you teach it)? What do you do during a typical week?**

My preparation is mostly hands-on. I spend my week solving past WorldSkills test projects against the clock: building WPF desktop apps, REST APIs and database schemes. I focus heavily on LINQ, async patterns and solid architecture so I can code quickly and cleanly under stress.

**Q2. How do you notice that a student, or you yourself, is struggling? At what point does it usually become visible?**

I know I'm stuck when I spend over an hour on a single bug or start using trial-and-error coding instead of a structured plan. During competition prep, wasting time like that is fatal, so recognizing that wall early is crucial.

**Q3. Has it happened that it was unclear which assessment criteria cost you points? How do you deal with that?**

It happens often. Sometimes a test project leaves requirement descriptions ambiguous, or auto-graders expect exact field names not clearly specified in the prompt. When I lose points inexplicably, I review the automated test logs with my mentor and strictly enforce standard naming conventions and edge-case handling in my code.

**Q4. Imagine a platform that shows the teacher a list of students who may need help, with the reasons. Would that be useful? What should such a list include?**

Yes, highly useful for mentors. It shouldn't just flag "student is behind", but show specific triggers: spending 45+ minutes on one task, repeated build or compilation errors, or failing the same test suite multiple times. That lets the teacher step in with direct, actionable help.

**Q5. The platform will record activity on the site: which pages you open, how long you spend, and your attempts at exercises. How do you feel about that, and under what conditions would you agree?**

I'm fine with tracking activity, attempts and page time if the data is strictly used to improve teaching and catch blockers early. I would agree to it on two conditions: complete transparency about what's tracked, and a guarantee that the data won't be used punitively.
