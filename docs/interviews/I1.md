## Questions and answers
The same five questions were asked in every interview ([Interview questions](interview-questions.md)).

**Q1. Could you tell us how you learn C# or prepare for WorldSkills (or how you teach it)? What do you do during a typical week?**

I mostly prepare by solving modules from past WorldSkills championships in C#: database design, then a WPF desktop app. During the week I do 2–3 evening sessions of a couple of hours with a timer, plus one longer session on the weekend where I try to do a full module under real conditions. When I get stuck on something like LINQ or data binding, I go back to Microsoft Docs or old code. Once a week our coach checks my solution against the marking scheme.

**Q2. How do you notice that a student, or you yourself, is struggling? At what point does it usually become visible?**

For me, the first sign is time: I keep running out of it on the same type of task, or I keep looking up the same thing. With other students it shows much later. They don't say they don't understand; they just skip practice or submit half a module. Usually the coach notices only at a mock competition, when it's already late.

**Q3. Has it happened that it was unclear which assessment criteria cost you points? How do you deal with that?**

Yes, several times. After a mock round I'd see the total score for a module but not which criteria I lost points on, especially the subjective ones like UI or code readability. Usually I ask the expert to go through the scheme with me or compare my work with a stronger solution. I also made my own checklist from the marking scheme.

**Q4. Imagine a platform that shows the teacher a list of students who may need help, with the reasons. Would that be useful? What should such a list include?**

I think it would be useful, because the teacher would see problems earlier. But it needs concrete reasons, not just an "at risk" label: which topic or module, how many failed attempts, when the student was last active, whether results are getting worse, and which criteria they usually lose points on. It would also be good if students could see their own version of this list.

**Q5. The platform will record activity on the site: which pages you open, how long you spend, and your attempts at exercises. How do you feel about that, and under what conditions would you agree?**

It's a bit uncomfortable, especially time tracking, because time on a page doesn't really show effort. I'd agree if it's clearly explained what is collected, only the teacher sees my data, it's not used for grading, it's anonymized for research, and I can see my own data too. Tracking exercise attempts is actually fine and useful, because it shows where I get stuck.
