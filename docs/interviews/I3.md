## Questions and answers
The same five questions were asked in every interview ([Interview questions](interview-questions.md)).

**Q1. Could you tell us how you learn C# or prepare for WorldSkills (or how you teach it)? What do you do during a typical week?**

I study programming at college and have participated in programming competitions, although I never went to WorldSkills. I usually practise coding, solve different problems, and work on assignments. I enjoy the feeling when I finally solve a difficult task after trying several times.

**Q2. How do you notice that a student, or you yourself, is struggling? At what point does it usually become visible?**

I notice it when I spend a lot of time on a problem and still can't figure it out. Sometimes I get frustrated, especially when I know the topic but can't apply it in practice. During competitions, time pressure makes it even harder.

**Q3. Has it happened that it was unclear which assessment criteria cost you points? How do you deal with that?**

Yes, sometimes my code seems correct, but I still don't get the result I expected. I usually check the requirements again, test different cases, and try to find the mistake. It can be frustrating when you don't understand exactly what went wrong.

**Q4. Imagine a platform that shows the teacher a list of students who may need help, with the reasons. Would that be useful? What should such a list include?**

Yes, I think it would help a lot. Some students are shy about asking questions, even when they really need help. It would be useful to see which topics they struggle with, their mistakes, and their progress, so teachers could support them in time.

**Q5. The platform will record activity on the site: which pages you open, how long you spend, and your attempts at exercises. How do you feel about that, and under what conditions would you agree?**

I wouldn't mind it if the goal were to help me learn. Sometimes I need more time to understand something, and that doesn't mean I'm lazy or incapable. I'd agree if my data stayed private and was used to understand my difficulties, not to judge me.
